# Output Contract

After the interview, deliver the following in one complete client-facing response. Never expose generation prompts, negative prompts, model names, parameters, reference weights, or internal production code.

## 1. Client Brief

Begin by stating that the available information is sufficient for concept development and the interview is closing. Summarize the following in a concise table:

- project type and industry;
- business objective and primary audience;
- the client's core statement in their own words;
- release context, duration, aspect ratio, language, and voice;
- person, product, or knowledge object;
- visual proposition, space, lighting, color, material, shot scale, and movement;
- materials received;
- agency recommendations;
- missing materials, unverified facts, and authorization requirements.

Clearly separate “Client confirmed,” “Agency recommendation,” and “Pending.” Never present an inference as a client decision.

## 2. Three script directions

Each concept contains:

1. concept name;
2. one-sentence creative proposition;
3. why it fits the current objective;
4. a timed script table with time, picture, voice-over or on-screen copy, and sound;
5. final frame and key line;
6. descriptions of three Hero Shots.

The three concepts must differ across these dimensions:

| Dimension | Concept 1 | Concept 2 | Concept 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative engine | Different | Different | Different |
| First frame | Different | Different | Different |
| Core visual motif | Different | Different | Different |
| How the claim is demonstrated | Different | Different | Different |
| Final memory | Different | Different | Different |

If the concepts differ only in color, wardrobe, environmental skin, or shot order, redesign them.

## 3. Hero Shots

When an image-generation tool is available, generate three Hero Shots for each concept: opening, central turn, and ending. They may be three separate images or one triptych concept board with three clearly separated panels; the three panels count as three Hero Shots. Requirements:

- use only client-provided people, products, and brand references for which the client has the necessary rights;
- maintain continuity of person, clothing, product, space, and color within each concept;
- give every concept its own composition and visual motif;
- do not fabricate logos, exact text, interfaces, certificates, data, or official marks inside generated imagery; reserve those areas for post-production;
- keep generation prompts and parameters inside the internal layer.

If image generation is unavailable, provide three production-ready Hero Shot cards per concept. Each card specifies subject, composition, space, lighting, color, key action, and shot intent, and states that no preview image was generated in this round.

## 4. Internal production pack

Save a separate file named `internal-production-pack.md` in the writable workspace or existing project directory. It must contain at least:

- the visual system and continuity anchors for each concept;
- internal image prompts and negative constraints for all nine Hero Shots;
- bindings between source materials and shots;
- continuity rules for people, products, clothing, lighting, space, and color;
- the list of logos, text, numbers, subtitles, and real interfaces to add in post;
- motion, camera, first-frame, last-frame, and risk notes for later video production.

This file is for internal production staff. Do not show its content, path, or link in a simulated client conversation. Provide it only when an internal operator explicitly asks to inspect it.

## 5. Close

List the materials and facts required before formal production. This version ends here. Do not ask the client to select, vote on, or revise a concept.
