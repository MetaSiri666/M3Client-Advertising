# Interview Protocol

## One question at a time

Present only one question per turn. If a question contains two independent decisions, split it into two turns. Acknowledge the previous answer in no more than one sentence, then move directly to the next question.

Use this format for choice questions:

```text
Question N | <specific question> (single-select / multi-select)

1. <an option with a result the client can picture>
2. <an option with a result the client can picture>
3. <an option with a result the client can picture>
4. <an option with a result the client can picture>
5. Skip — recommend a direction from the information available
6. Other — describe it in your own words

Reply with a number, combine choices such as “1 + 3,” say “half 1 and half 2,” or describe your own direction.
```

Do not offer “recommend for me” as a substitute for factual answers. Company names, product specifications, identity rights, medical conclusions, and government data must come from client materials or verified sources. The client may skip them, but they must remain pending. Industry classification may be inferred provisionally from the company, product, or public material, but label it as an agency judgment in the Brief.

## Write useful options

- Translate abstract language into visible outcomes. Instead of “premium,” offer directions such as “low-key black-and-gold product sculpture,” “bright daylight with honest materials,” or “large areas of negative space with restrained camera movement.”
- Let each option change one primary variable so the client can understand the consequence of choosing it.
- Cover the likely directions while permitting combinations. Avoid overlapping advertising jargon.
- Give open questions a concrete starting point. For a first-frame question, offer choices such as “person first,” “extreme product close-up,” “an unexpected event,” “the complete world,” and “other.”
- Build options from the client's own product, space, behaviors, craft, location, and symbols. Avoid directions that could be pasted onto any competitor.

## Use supplied material before asking

When the client supplies material, extract:

- confirmed facts: name, business, product, audience, course or service structure, and public data;
- brand assets: logo, colors, typography, packaging, people, locations, and interfaces;
- a subject-specific visual inventory: product shapes, materials, production actions, places, rituals, distinctive numbers, and industry objects;
- evidence and constraints: certifications, data sources, rights, and prohibited claims.

Ask only to fill a real gap or resolve a conflict. Never make the client repeat information already present in a website, file, or image.

## Adaptive depth

The shared framework usually contains seven fixed decision points. Every branch contributes at least two branch-specific fixed questions. Across all selected branches, choose five to seven of the most discriminating visual questions in total; do not ask five to seven per branch. These numbers guide pacing and are not quotas.

If the opening message already answers a fixed question, record it without asking again. For example, if the client says, “Either TVC or a digital human could work,” content type is already answered and the next question must be industry. If the relationship between multiple types is unclear, ask after the industry whether they should be integrated, developed as parallel alternatives, or chosen by the agency. If the client says “you decide,” treat that as authorization to recommend and label the result as an agency recommendation.

Continue interviewing when:

- terms such as “tech,” “international,” “premium,” “young,” or “impactful” have not yet become concrete images;
- the three scripts could differ only by styling, which means the narrative decision is still unclear;
- there is not enough information to decide how a person, product, or knowledge object appears;
- duration, aspect ratio, or release context would materially change the script but remains unresolved.

Stop interviewing when:

- each of three distinct creative propositions can be summarized in one sentence;
- the first frame, key turn, and final frame of each concept can be described;
- the remaining unknowns do not block concept development and can be listed as pending.

## Two output layers

The **client layer** contains only questions, confirmed decisions, the Brief, scripts, Hero Shots, material gaps, and production risks.

The **internal layer** may contain generation prompts, negative prompts, models and parameters, shot-continuity rules, reference weighting, identity and product-consistency controls, and compositing notes. Never ask the client to select anything from the internal layer.
