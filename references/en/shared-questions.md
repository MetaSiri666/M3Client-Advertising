# Shared Question Framework

These are the fixed decision points for every project. The first and second decisions must remain in this order. Adapt the wording and options for decisions three through seven, while asking only one question per turn.

## 1. Content type

```text
What type of content are we primarily creating? (Multi-select is allowed, and you may specify priorities.)

1. TVC advertising film
2. AI digital human
3. AI science or educational animation
4. AI government or enterprise promotional film
5. Other — describe it in your own words
```

If the client selects several types, ask the industry question next. Only after the industry is known should one separate question establish whether the types will be integrated, developed as parallel alternatives, or reduced to an agency-recommended primary type. When the client has already said “you decide,” do not ask again; recommend a primary type from the objective and mark it in the Brief.

## 2. Client industry

```text
Which industry best describes your company or project? (Single-select; choose several if the project crosses industries.)

1. Consumer goods, retail, or lifestyle services
2. Technology, internet, AI, or software
3. Manufacturing, industrial, energy, or automotive
4. Finance, real estate, or professional services
5. Education, culture, media, or tourism
6. Medical, health, or life sciences
7. Government, industrial park, city, or public services
8. Other — describe it in your own words
9. Unsure — infer it from my company or product materials
```

Industry is a routing decision. If the client selects “Other,” ask for the industry name in the next turn. If the client is unsure or skips, make a provisional classification from their company, product, or materials. If there is not enough evidence, mark the industry pending while treating the second decision point as complete.

## 3. Business objective

Rewrite the options for the selected content type. Usually cover: build brand recognition, explain a product or service, establish trust, generate inquiries or enrollment, launch a new initiative, support internal reporting, or communicate with the public. Allow multiple selections and prioritization.

Do not ask the vague question “What effect do you want?” Ask what change the film must create in the audience.

## 4. Primary audience

List real roles from the client's industry, such as enterprise decision-makers, technical leads, channel partners, families, teenagers, the general public, government stakeholders, or investors. Allow multiple selections and ask for the first-priority audience only when that choice would change the concept.

## 5. Core content or single memory

When the client already has a clear statement, ask them to express it in their own words. Offer four starting points: a product capability, a brand value, a concrete outcome, or an emotional impression. Preserve the client's original wording in the Brief before turning it into advertising copy.

## 6. Primary viewing context

Adapt the options to the project. Common contexts include a website or sales presentation, short-form social feed, trade show or launch screen, pitch or internal presentation, store or public space, and training or classroom use. The primary context controls the first three seconds, subtitle density, pacing, and aspect ratio.

## 7. Desired action after viewing

Use concrete outcomes: remember the brand, understand a concept, trust a capability, make an inquiry, enroll or purchase, share the film, or support an initiative. Include “build an impression without requiring an immediate action.”

## Additional delivery questions

Ask only for information that is still unknown and would change the script:

- duration;
- aspect ratio;
- language and subtitles;
- voice or speaker identity;
- number of versions;
- deadline.

Continue to ask one item per turn. If the client is unsure, recommend an option suited to the viewing context and explain the outcome rather than internal production parameters.

## Visual question pool

Do not ask “What style do you like?” Select the five to seven variables that matter most for this project:

- what appears in the first frame;
- the space surrounding the subject;
- where the light comes from and how strong the contrast is;
- whether the main color comes from the brand, environment, or story object;
- whether the material language feels natural, industrial, digital, handcrafted, or graphic;
- how close the camera is to the subject;
- whether the camera observes steadily, pushes in slowly, travels rapidly, or cuts rhythmically;
- whether the space feels dense and full or restrained with negative space;
- how many people appear, in which roles, and performing what actions;
- which common industry images the client explicitly rejects.

Every question must offer concrete visual options plus “Skip / recommend for me.” Never stop at adjectives such as “premium,” “cinematic,” or “technological.”
