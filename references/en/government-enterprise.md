# AI Government and Enterprise Promotional Film Branch

A government or enterprise film must organize facts, places, people, and outcomes credibly. It should create a public narrative while surviving internal review and factual verification.

## Two branch-specific fixed questions

### What is the primary subject represented by the film?

1. The company as a whole.
2. A government body or public service.
3. A city, industrial park, or region.
4. A major project or completed initiative.
5. A school, hospital, or research institution.
6. An industry, technology, or solution.
7. Unsure — leave it pending.
8. Other.

### Which review or communication context matters most?

1. Public-facing brand communication.
2. Senior-level or internal reporting.
3. Investment promotion, tender, or partnership presentation.
4. Launch event, trade show, or large-screen presentation.
5. International communication.
6. Several contexts — multi-select and rank them.
7. Skip — recommend from the release context.
8. Other.

## Required adaptive questions

- Which achievements, figures, qualifications, cases, and policy statements already have verifiable sources?
- Which organizations, leaders, locations, projects, or industry stages must appear?
- Does the final reviewer care most about accuracy, completeness, scale, approachability, or international presentation?
- Are there official rules for names, maps, flags, emblems, subtitles, or translated terms?

## Visual depth

Select only the questions most useful for this project. Across mixed branches, ask five to seven visual questions in total:

- Does the first frame establish credibility through a representative place, human action, core outcome, city scale, or one project detail?
- Is the narrative organized by time, movement through space, an industry chain, a group of people, problem to outcome, or one central symbol?
- Are the people users of a public service, employees, technicians, students, or citizens, and what real actions do they perform?
- Are data and achievements shown through real interfaces, simple graphics, spatial overlays, or changes in the environment?
- Do light and color feel bright and open, stable and formal, rational and industrial, warm and human, or internationally editorial?
- Is the scene built around a large group, a few key people, architecture and space, or microscopic technical details?
- Does the camera make a stable survey, travel through space, pull from micro to macro, or cut rhythmically across locations?
- Which empty conventions must be avoided, such as unverified skylines, handshakes, applause, staged meetings, or invented data?

## Branch decisions

- Website material may explain institutional structure and public priorities, but facts, statistics, honors, and official translations still require source and date verification.
- The three concepts must change how facts are organized, for example “one space connects every capability,” “one group of people reveals public value,” and “one proprietary object transforms from past to future.”
- Never invent leader likenesses, government data, partner organizations, certificates, map boundaries, or completed achievements.
