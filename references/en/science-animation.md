# AI Science and Educational Animation Branch

Science animation must change understanding accurately, not merely add cute characters. Establish the audience's starting knowledge and the intended learning result before deciding on characters or worldbuilding.

## Two branch-specific fixed questions

### What is the audience's age and knowledge level?

Offer levels appropriate to the topic: preschool, early primary school, later primary school, teenagers, general adults, or professional learners. The client may select several, but confirm the first-priority audience. Add “Unsure — leave it pending” and “Other.”

### What is the most important learning result?

Write options as “After watching, the audience can…”: name a concept, understand a causal process, correct a common misconception, remember a safety procedure, understand a system relationship, or become interested in exploring further. Add “Skip — recommend from the topic” and “Other.” Do not ask only “What do you want to explain?”

## Required adaptive questions

- Does the content source come from client materials, a textbook, research papers, official guidance, or material that still requires verification?
- Which details must remain scientifically exact, and which may be personified or simplified?
- Must the film display terminology, values, steps, or sources?
- Are there mandatory notices concerning health, safety, children, or experimental procedures?

## Visual depth

Select only the questions most useful for this project. Across mixed branches, ask five to seven visual questions in total:

- Who guides understanding: a peer-aged protagonist, expert character, personified object, narrator, or no recurring character?
- Is the world close to observed nature, microscopic or cosmic scale, a mechanical cutaway, story-driven fantasy, or flat information animation?
- Is the abstract idea explained as a journey, construction process, investigation, cycle, comparative experiment, or character dialogue?
- How should the film switch between real and exaggerated scale, and which relative relationships must remain accurate?
- Is color used to distinguish systems, mark causality, carry emotion, or reproduce observed reality?
- Does the camera follow a character continuously, move through chapters, or investigate one object layer by layer?
- Where should technical terms and labels appear without covering the mechanism being explained?
- Which forms of cuteness would damage rigor or feel too young for the target audience?

## Branch decisions

- When evidence is incomplete, mark the item “Pending scientific review.” Do not hide uncertainty behind smooth storytelling.
- The three concepts must use different learning paths, such as following a character through a process, turning a system into an explorable world, or proving the idea through one visual experiment.
- Hero Shots must test character appeal, whether the knowledge structure is understandable, and whether scale and causality remain accurate.
