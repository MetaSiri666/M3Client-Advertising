# AI Digital Human Branch

A digital-human project depends on a credible identity, clear communication, and consistency over time. It is not a standard TVC with a talking character added.

## Two branch-specific fixed questions

### What is the source of the digital identity?

1. Replicate a real person.
2. Design an original person from the brand.
3. Create a long-term host or expert IP character.
4. Create a non-human brand character.
5. Unsure — leave it pending for now.
6. Other.

When the client chooses a real person, separately confirm that the client has the right to use that person's likeness and supplied materials. Concept work may continue before confirmation, but authorization is a formal-production prerequisite.

### What role does the digital human play in the content?

1. Company founder or executive.
2. Technical or professional expert.
3. Brand host.
4. Product guide or customer-service representative.
5. Instructor or knowledge presenter.
6. Skip — recommend a role from the objective.
7. Other.

## Required adaptive questions

Complete the following one at a time when relevant. Appearance, wardrobe, performance, and visual-space preferences must include “Skip — recommend for me” and “Other.” Voice authorization, identity rights, and real identity are factual questions; they may be skipped only as pending items.

- Should the appearance be a high-fidelity replica, a lightly refined version, or a stylized character that preserves identity cues?
- Should the wardrobe suggest a formal executive, relaxed technology founder, professional uniform, branded styling, or another role?
- Should the voice be an authorized clone, an independent synthetic voice with an appropriate age and status, or client-supplied audio?
- Should the performance be stable front-facing delivery, half-body delivery with restrained gestures, walking presentation, or interaction with a virtual object?
- What is the primary language, and are separate complete versions required for different languages?

## Visual depth

Select only the questions most useful for this project. Across mixed branches, ask five to seven visual questions in total:

- In the first second, does the digital human look directly at camera, operate an object, emerge from the environment, or appear after a product problem is introduced?
- Is the setting a bright office, restrained launch stage, digital reconstruction of an industry site, branded studio, or abstract information space?
- Do person and product information sit side by side, in foreground and background, as a tabletop model, as a spatial projection, or as a world the person enters?
- Is a complex technology best explained through a digital twin, cutaway, data flow, process route, scale change, or before-and-after state?
- Is the light natural daylight, controlled stage light, even laboratory light, or projection in brand colors?
- Does the camera remain stable for credibility or enter a virtual world at key statements?
- How dense is the background information, and how much of the frame does the person occupy?
- Which failure modes must be avoided: stiff delivery, over-retouching, sales tone, false smiling, exaggerated gestures, or something else?

## Branch decisions

- One low-resolution portrait supports only concept-level likeness. For formal high-fidelity work, request high-resolution front and side views, natural expressions, and varied lighting, while allowing the client to continue the concept stage without them.
- An independent synthetic voice may match the person's age and professional presence but must not be described as that person's real voice.
- The three concepts must change how the presenter makes complex information visible, for example by entering a digital system, controlling a tabletop model, or turning a drawing into an operating environment, rather than changing only the office background.
